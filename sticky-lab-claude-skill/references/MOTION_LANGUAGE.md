# Sticky Lab — Motion Language

## Core motion principle
Movement should feel like a person arranging paper objects on a desk: purposeful, tactile, slightly imperfect, and easy to follow. It is stop-motion-inspired, not uncontrolled jitter.

## Approved motion verbs
Slide, place, peel, flip, fold, unfold, pin, tape, tear-reveal, underline, circle, draw-on, stack, fan-out, nudge, rotate slightly, settle, lift a corner, reveal from beneath, swap, connect with a hand-drawn arrow.

## Motion rules
1. Each scene has one primary motion event and at most two supporting events.
2. Give objects a clear start state, movement path, and end state.
3. Use short, controlled easing. A tiny overshoot is allowed only when it feels like paper settling, never a rubbery bounce.
4. Use slight rotations and imperfect alignment sparingly. Do not make all notes wobble continuously.
5. Hand-drawn lines should reveal as if drawn by a marker, following the line path.
6. Text can appear by being uncovered, written on, stamped onto a note, or revealed with a paper movement. Avoid generic typing effects unless the content calls for it.
7. Transitions should preserve the same tabletop/notebook world: a page turn, paper slide, note covering the lens, or a connected rearrangement of existing objects.
8. Avoid frequent camera zooms, whip pans, liquid morphs, random flying cards, particle bursts, and unrelated transition presets.
9. Camera movement is optional. Prefer a stable overhead or slight angled tabletop view; use a slow, motivated push-in only for emphasis.
10. A scene must settle into a readable composition before the next major information reveal.

## Suggested timing ranges (guides, not laws)
- Small note slide/place: 0.25–0.55 s
- Tape press or corner fold: 0.20–0.45 s
- Marker draw-on: 0.25–0.80 s depending on line length
- Keyword reveal: 0.20–0.45 s
- Paper flip / cover transition: 0.35–0.70 s
- Settle / readability hold: 0.40–1.20 s

Use timing that fits the narration and total duration. Do not force all motions to the same duration.

## Stop-motion feel
- Use discrete, intentional placements and tiny positional changes.
- The texture can feel tactile, but the output should remain visually stable and legible.
- No constant jitter, camera shake, random drift, or frame-rate stutter.
- If rendered at 30 fps, emulate stop motion through designed stepped movement or selective held poses, not by accidentally lowering the whole video's frame rate.

## Audio behavior
- Sound effects are optional and restrained: soft paper slide, tape peel, marker stroke, light paper tap.
- Avoid a sound effect for every object. Use sound to reinforce the most meaningful action.
- Music stays under narration and should not dictate random motion.
